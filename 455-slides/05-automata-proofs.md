# Summary of Concepts Learned so Far

We learned a *regular language* is something:

<v-clicks>
    
- recognized by a DFA
- recognized by an NFA (Recall that DFAs and NFAs are computationally equivalent!)
- generated by a regular expression

</v-clicks>

<v-clicks>


If you had to prove a language is regular, you can use a *proof by construction*, building a DFA, NFA, or regular expression to represent that language.

</v-clicks>

---

# Proving Properties About Automata via Construction

Let's prove the following:

Assume $A$ and $B$ are regular languages. Then $A \cap B$ is also
regular.

<v-clicks>

Approach: A proof by construction.

If $A$ and $B$ are regular, then there exist automata

$M_1 = (Q_1, \Sigma, \delta_1, s_1, F_1)$

$M_2 = (Q_2, \Sigma, \delta_2, s_2, F_2)$

With $L(M_1) = A$ and $L(M_2) = B$.

Two elements of the proof:

- Construction of automaton $M_3$
- Proof that $L(M_3) = L(M_1) \cap L(M_2)$

</v-clicks>

---

# Construction of $M_3$

Construction

$L(M_1) = A$ and $L(M_2) = B$.

$M_1 = (Q_1, \Sigma, \delta_1, s_1, F_1)$

$M_2 = (Q_2, \Sigma, \delta_2, s_2, F_2)$

Let $M_3 = (Q_3, \Sigma, \delta_3, s_3, F_3)$ with: 

<v-clicks>

* $Q_3 = Q_1 \times Q_2 = \{(p,q) \mid p \in Q_1, q \in Q_2 \}$

* $\delta_3((p,q),d) = (\delta_1(p,d), \delta_2(q,d))$
    
* $s_3 = (s_1,s_2)$

* $F_3 = F_1 \times F_2$


</v-clicks>

<v-clicks>

What we have:

$L(M_1) = A$ and $L(M_2) = B$.

$M_2 = (Q_2, \Sigma, \delta_2, s_2, F_2)$

</v-clicks>

# Proof of Correctness

Now we need to prove $L(M_3) = L(M_1) \cap L(M_2)$.

we restate this by saying "any string in $L(M_3)$ is also in $L(M_1) \cap L(M_2)$" or 

$$\forall x \in \Sigma^*, x \in L(M_3) \iff x \in L(M_1) \cap L(M_2)$$

# Proofs with Regular Expressions

Last lesson:

Let $A \subseteq \Sigma^*$. 
$A$ is a regular language
$\iff$
$A = L(\alpha)$ for some regular expression $\alpha$

Let's go ahead and prove it!

We have to prove this in both directions:

* $\to$ If $A = L(\alpha)$ for some regular expression $\alpha$, then $A$ is a regular language

* $\leftarrow$ If $A$ is a regular language, then $A = L(\alpha)$ for some regular expression $\alpha$
    

---




# Proving $\to$ direction

Want to Prove:

If $A = L(\alpha)$ for some regular expression $\alpha$, then $A$ is a regular language.

We can do this with a proof by induction!

We can use the (non redundant) atomic patterns for our base case $a$, $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}$, and $\boldsymbol{\emptyset}$

and the (non redundant) compound patterns
$\cup$, $\circ$, and $*$


**Base Cases:** $\{\{a\}, \boldsymbol{\epsilon}, \boldsymbol{\emptyset}\}$

# Inductive Step: $\{\cup,\circ,* \}$


 Let our statement hold for  smaller patterns $\beta$ and $\gamma$. (Our inductive hypothesis)

 More specifically, we use the IH that if $B = L(\beta)$ and $C = L(\gamma)$  for some pattern $\alpha$, then $B$ and $C$ are regular languages.

 Since every pattern $\alpha$ can be expressed in the following way: 
* $\beta \cup \gamma$
* $\beta \circ \gamma$
* $\beta^*$ 


More specifically, our inductive step is in three parts: 

* $L(\beta \cup \gamma)$ is a regular language
* $L(\beta \circ \gamma)$ is a regular language
* $L(\beta^*)$ is a regular language



As you can see this is a *proof by cases*. 

For the sake of time, we will not prove each of these, but for each case, you will do a *proof by construction*. 

---

# $L(\beta \cup \gamma)$ is a regular language

Let's prove the first case: $L(\beta \cup \gamma)$ is a regular language.


| Proof Step | Reasoning |
| --- | --- |
| <span v-click="1"> 1. $B = L(\beta)$ and  $C = L(\gamma)$ are regular languages</span>| <span v-click="1"> Inductive Hypothesis  </span> |
| <span v-click="2"> 2. $L(\beta \cup \gamma) = L(\beta) \cup L(\gamma)$ </span>| <span v-click="2"> Compound Pattern Definition </span> |

# Proving $\leftarrow$

If $A$ is a regular language, then $A = L(\alpha)$ for some regular expression $\alpha$

This is essentially saying each language accepted by a finite automaton can be represented by an equivalent regular expression.

So, we can prove this by converting a general finite automaton $N$ to an equivalent regular expression! (A proof by construction!)

---

# Construction

To convert a finite automaton to a regular expression, we use the state elimination method. We add a new single start and single accept state, then remove out the internal states one at a time, updating the labels on the remaining edges with the formula
$$\Delta(q_i, q_j) \;\leftarrow\; \Delta(q_i, q_j) \;\cup\; \Delta(q_i, q_{\text{rem}}) \cdot \Delta(q_{\text{rem}}, q_{\text{rem}})^* \cdot \Delta(q_{\text{rem}}, q_j).$$
When only the new start and new accept state remain, the single edge between them is labeled with a regular expression for $L(M)$.

---

# Example: Strings ending in 00

Let $M$ be the following DFA:

<img src="/public/step1.png" width="400"/>


Step 1: Add a new start and a new accept state


---



# Step 2: Remove $q_1$


$$
\begin{align*}
\Delta(q_0, q_0) &\leftarrow \Delta(q_0, q_0) \cup \Delta(q_0, q_1) \cdot \Delta(q_1, q_1)^* \cdot \Delta(q_1, q_0) = 1 \cup 0 \cdot \varepsilon \cdot 1 = 1 \cup 01,\\
\Delta(q_0, q_2) &\leftarrow \Delta(q_0, q_2) \cup \Delta(q_0, q_1) \cdot \Delta(q_1, q_1)^* \cdot \Delta(q_1, q_2) = \emptyset \cup 0 \cdot \varepsilon \cdot 0 = 00.
\end{align*}
$$


<img src="/public/step2.png" width="400"/>

---


# Step 3: Remove $q_0$


$$
\begin{align*}
\Delta(s, q_2) &\leftarrow \Delta(s, q_2) \cup \Delta(s, q_0) \cdot (1 \cup 01)^* \cdot \Delta(q_0, q_2)\\
&= \emptyset \cup \varepsilon \cdot (1 \cup 01)^* \cdot 00 = (1 \cup 01)^* 00,\\
\Delta(q_2, q_2) &\leftarrow \Delta(q_2, q_2) \cup \Delta(q_2, q_0) \cdot (1 \cup 01)^* \cdot \Delta(q_0, q_2)\\
&= 0 \cup 1 \cdot (1 \cup 01)^* \cdot 00 = 0 \cup 1(1 \cup 01)^* 00.
\end{align*}
$$


<img src="/public/step3.png" width="400"/>

---

# Step 4: Remove $q_2$

$$
\begin{align*}
\Delta(s, f) &\leftarrow \emptyset \cup (1 \cup 01)^* 00 \cdot \bigl(0 \cup 1(1 \cup 01)^* 00\bigr)^* \cdot \varepsilon\\
&= (1 \cup 01)^* 00 \bigl(0 \cup 1(1 \cup 01)^* 00\bigr)^*.
\end{align*}
$$

<img src="/public/step4.png" width="400"/>

---

### Final Answer

$$L(M) = L\Bigl((1 \cup 01)^* \, 00 \, \bigl(0 \cup 1(1 \cup 01)^* 00\bigr)^*\Bigr).$$

# Proof of Correctness

Technically, to make this a complete proof, we need to prove this conversion works. 

So we must prove $L(M)$ is equivalent to the set generated by the resulting regular expression. We will skip the details of this proof, but in summary it's a proof by induction, showing this state elimination strategy works when removing one state from a 2-state automaton, and then showing the same approach works for an automaton with $n$ states.
